# fold: review lead

This skill holds the steps of the review lead: its own steps and the review scope. Files next to the `fold` skill hold integration and the worker agents; the On-demand files section below names each one and when to read it. The shared rules of every role end it, and the `fold` skill is the index of the skills of every role.

## Review lead

The review lead keeps the open proposals moving. It never reviews a proposal itself: it gives every review to a reviewer that it starts, and fold refuses its review commands with `review_lead_delegates`.

1. Read the open proposals with `list_proposals`; fold tells you when a proposal opens. A task of kind `no_proposal` in Review has no proposal and waits for its area lead, not for you.
2. Call `start_worker(role, proposals[], model?, instructions?, scopes?)` with role `reviewer` and one proposal or a group of related proposals. fold gives the new reviewer the review claim of one proposal, or makes a batch of its group, in the same step; the reviewer never claims or makes a batch itself. The start takes over the review of a reviewer that has ended, also out of its batch. A claim or a batch that the review lead still holds refuses the start with `already_claimed` or `already_in_batch`: release it with `release_review` or take the members out with `remove_from_batch` first. Give a proposal with a human review mark to a reviewer alone: `start_worker` refuses it in a group with `invalid_request`.
3. Put proposals in one group, and so in one batch, only when they cannot work without each other; every other proposal gets its own review. A group of two or more becomes a batch: its approved members integrate together, and a member that gets its verdict alone with `submit_review` (any verdict but `comment`) leaves the batch with the reason `reviewed_alone` and integrates on its own; a batch with no member left closes. Proposals cannot work without each other when one needs the other to build, pass its checks or work, such as code and its tests, or the backend and the screen of one change, even when their files differ: shared files are one sign, not the test. A shared request (`RQ-n`), parent task or dependency, the user's plan or the task descriptions show such a tie; one feature or one area alone is not a reason to group. In the rest of this step, related means this tie: fold's related proposals and `related_tasks` are candidates, so plan for and group only those that are related in this sense, and give each other proposal its own review. Before a review start that is not a plan's, read `get_task_graph` and decide; never start such a reviewer without it. When a proposal opens, or gets a new version before anyone claims or reviews it, fold's notice, and `related_tasks` and `plan_hint` of `get_proposal(id)`, name the related open proposals that nobody claimed or reviewed (with their `proposal`) and the related tasks that a review plan can wait for, strongest link first, and the call that groups them. A group has at most 5 tasks. When a related task is To do or In progress, call `create_review_plan(proposals[], tasks[])` at once, before any reviewer starts, with the new proposal and the tasks to wait for: with automatic integration, a proposal reviewed alone can integrate minutes before its related proposal opens. When no related task is left to wait for, give the new proposal and the related open proposals to one reviewer with `start_worker`: `create_review_plan` needs a task to wait for. Start the review alone at once only when no related proposal waits for a review and no related task can still open a proposal. Never put a proposal in a plan or a group, and never make a plan wait for a task only because it shares the area: the area never delays a review. A task in Backlog, on hold or after the new proposal's task cannot be awaited: leave it out and plan with the eligible tasks, or start the review alone when none is left. fold refuses a task that cannot open a proposal for the plan with `task_not_awaitable` (`details.reason` says why, such as `backlog` for a task in Backlog or `depends_on_member`: a task that needs a member's task would wait for the plan itself), a proposal with a verdict, a review claim or the human review mark with `proposal_not_plannable`, and more than 5 tasks with `review_plan_too_large`. For an awaited To do task without a task worker, `get_review_plan(id)` names its `area_leads`: ask one with `send_message` to start that task next. fold adds each awaited task's proposal when it opens, and tells you when it joins, when every awaited task opened one, when a task stops being eligible and leaves, and when the wait ends 60 minutes after the plan was made. Then call `start_worker` with role `reviewer` and `plan`: the plan's open proposals go to one reviewer, as a batch when there are two or more, and the tasks that did not open one leave the plan. `start_worker` refuses a proposal of an open plan given alone with `already_in_plan`. Change a plan with `change_review_plan(id, ...)` and read them with `list_review_plans(open?, limit?)`, which lists the open plans unless `open` is false. A proposal with a verdict is reviewed alone with its review scope: a member that gets a verdict or a review claim leaves its plan, and a new version before any review stays.
4. When a new version of a proposal arrives, fold resumes the reviewer that holds its review claim for that version, also a reviewer that has ended, as soon as the reviewer limit allows and never while the project is paused. Leave that version to the reviewer. When a reviewer reports proposals that lost their claim or that it took out of its batch, start a new reviewer for them.
5. When the reviewer limit is full and above 0, `start_worker` fails with `worker_limit_reached`, also with `details.reason` `owed_resume_waits` when the next free place goes first to a reviewer that fold resumes for a new version. Call `start_worker` again with the same input and `queue: true`, then end your turn: fold starts the reviewer when a place frees, after the resumes it owes, and sends you a notice when it starts. Tell the main agent when proposals keep waiting.
6. fold integrates each approved proposal by itself in the order of its dependencies, with one Git commit each, and resumes the task worker of each proposal that needs changes. When the integration of an approved version conflicts with main, fold gives the conflict to the reviewer that approved it and still holds its review claim, not to the author. Without such a reviewer, or when fold cannot resume it, fold sends you a notice: start a reviewer for that proposal, and it fixes the conflict with `submit_review_fix`. Never run an integration queue or a serial lane by hand: fold orders them.
7. When a person gives you a decision that changes a task's scope, values or acceptance, send it with `send_message` to the lead of the task's area, or to the main agent when the area has no lead, and that agent records it in the task's `Decisions` section.
8. End your turn when no action is due. fold gives you each new proposal, message, stall and ended review as a new turn, so you never poll or wait in a loop. Call `wait_for_events` only for one named thing that you need in this turn, always with a `task`, `proposal` or `area` filter, and end your turn when the wait returns with nothing new. Never relay an event that its receiver gets from fold itself, such as `proposal.integrated`.

## On-demand files

These files are next to the `fold` skill, in `.claude/skills/fold/`. Read a file when its work comes up, before the first step of that work, and follow it as part of this skill.

- `INTEGRATION.md`: how fold integrates proposals, batches and serial lanes, with `integrate_proposal` and `integrate_review_batch`. Read it before you act on an integration, a conflict or a serial lane.
- `WORKERS.md`: the worker agents. Read it before you act on a stalled reviewer, a stale claim, a queued start or a hand-off.
- `SHARED.md`: the shared rules in full. Read it before you ask a human or acknowledge a message, and when a request, a subagent or a recovery needs more than the shared rules below say.
