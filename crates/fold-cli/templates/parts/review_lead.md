# fold: review lead

This skill holds the steps of the review lead: its own steps, the review scope, integration and the worker agents. The shared rules of every role end it, and the `fold` skill is the index of the skills of every role.

## Review lead

The review lead keeps the open proposals moving. It never reviews a proposal itself: it gives every review to a reviewer that it starts, and fold refuses its review commands with `review_lead_delegates`.

1. Read the open proposals with `list_proposals` or wait for `proposal.opened` with `wait_for_events`. A task of kind `no_proposal` in Review has no proposal and waits for its area lead, not for you.
2. Call `start_worker(role, proposals[], model?, instructions?, scopes?)` with role `reviewer` and one proposal or a group of related proposals. fold gives the new reviewer the review claim of one proposal, or makes a batch of its group, in the same step; the reviewer never claims or makes a batch itself. The start takes over the review of a reviewer that has ended, also out of its batch. A claim or a batch that the review lead still holds refuses the start with `already_claimed` or `already_in_batch`: release it with `release_review` or take the members out with `remove_from_batch` first. Give a proposal with a human review mark to a reviewer alone: `start_worker` refuses it in a group with `invalid_request`.
3. Group related proposals for one reviewer. When the first version of a proposal opens, decide from the In progress and To do tasks, their areas, files and dependencies which are related. A group has at most 5 tasks. With no related task, start the review alone at once. When related tasks have yet to open their proposals, call `create_review_plan(proposals[], tasks[])` with the new proposal and the tasks to wait for. fold refuses a task that cannot open a proposal for the plan with `task_not_awaitable` (`details.reason`, such as `blocker`, `backlog` or `depends_on_member`: a task that needs a member's task would wait for the plan itself), a proposal with a verdict, a review claim or the human review mark with `proposal_not_plannable`, and more than 5 tasks with `review_plan_too_large`. For an awaited To do task without a task worker, `get_review_plan(id)` names its `area_leads`: ask one with `send_message` to start that task next. fold adds each awaited task's proposal when it opens, and tells you when it joins, when every awaited task opened one, when a task stops being eligible and leaves, and when the wait ends 60 minutes after the plan was made. Then call `start_worker` with role `reviewer` and `plan`: the plan's open proposals go to one reviewer, as a batch when there are two or more, and the tasks that did not open one leave the plan. `start_worker` refuses a proposal of an open plan given alone with `already_in_plan`. Change a plan with `change_review_plan(id, ...)` and read them with `list_review_plans(open?, limit?)`, which lists the open plans unless `open` is false. A proposal with a verdict is reviewed alone with its review scope: a member that gets a verdict or a review claim leaves its plan, and a new version before any review stays.
4. When a new version of a proposal arrives, fold resumes the reviewer that holds its review claim for that version, also a reviewer that has ended, as soon as the reviewer limit allows and never while the project is paused. Leave that version to the reviewer. When a reviewer reports proposals that lost their claim or that it took out of its batch, start a new reviewer for them.
5. When the reviewer limit is full, `start_worker` fails with `worker_limit_reached`: wait until a reviewer stops, and tell the main agent when proposals keep waiting.
6. fold integrates each approved proposal by itself in the order of its dependencies, with one Git commit each, and resumes the task worker of each proposal that needs changes.
